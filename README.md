# Khaezulvoryn

> A crown of blue clay waits in the furnace for the winter that forged its king.

## The Potter's Last Shelf

The potter makes bowls with no bottoms and cups that cannot be emptied. People buy them for occasions they cannot explain. A woman keeps a bowl beside an unopened letter. A fisherman drinks from a cup before walking inland. Neither has asked for another.

At the back of the workshop stands a shelf covered with blue dust. On it rests a clay crown, still soft despite the years it has waited there. Its points lean inward. Between them gathers a little snow that melts whenever anyone tries to count the points.

The potter covers the crown before lighting his furnace. He says clay must never see the fire that has been prepared for it. When asked how he knows, he shows the pale marks around his wrists and returns to his wheel.

## A Commission Paid in Water

A visitor arrived carrying a jar sealed with black wax. She wanted a vessel large enough to hold what the jar contained, yet small enough to hide inside her hand. The potter placed it against the light and saw a single drop of water suspended above a tiny staircase.

She said the drop had fallen from the roof of a house buried beneath snow. The house belonged to someone she had promised to visit. Every road toward it had become a road leading away.

The potter asked whether the staircase rose or descended. She could not say. In her dreams, it did both, depending on which hand held the jar.

He set it beneath the covered crown. The wax softened. Above them, a small patch of snow appeared on the workshop ceiling without falling to the floor.

## The Clay That Remembered Skin

He worked through the afternoon, shaping blue clay around an empty space. The vessel looked ordinary from one side. From the other, its curved surface resembled a shoulder beneath folded cloth.

The visitor recognized the curve. She remembered a winter coat, a hand resting against a gate, and someone turning away before she had finished speaking. The memory stopped at the gate. Beyond it lay only white light.

The potter pressed his thumb against the clay. A scale appeared beneath its surface, followed by another, then a line of pale ridges that circled the vessel. From the furnace came a slow exhalation, though no fire had yet been lit.

He lifted the cloth from the crown. Its snow had become a little blue flame. The points now leaned toward the jar, and the staircase inside it cast a shadow across the entire workbench.

## Beneath the Furnace Floor

The furnace had been built over the mouth of a sleeping creature. Its teeth lay beneath the stone floor. Its horns extended into the hills behind the workshop, where sheep refused to graze after sunset.

The potter had discovered it as a child, when his father asked him to fetch ash. Looking through the grate, he had seen an eye rather than a fire. The eye contained a house beneath snow, and at its door stood a person holding a crown.

He had spent his life trying to make that crown. Every attempt had hardened before he could finish. Only the blue clay remained soft. Only the blue clay remembered the warmth of the eye.

The visitor looked into the furnace. Beyond its dark interior she saw the gate from her memory, standing open. Snow lay on one side. On the other, a bare hand waited above a latch.

## Letters Left by a Thumb

The potter's marks along the vessel had become writing:

*Khae — zul — voryn — asheth.*

The visitor traced the letters. The jar opened without breaking its seal. The drop rose into the blue vessel and spread across its inner wall, revealing the staircase as a line of steps cut into clear ice.

She understood that the house had not moved farther away. The road had been waiting for her to approach without carrying the last words she had never finished.

She spoke those words into the vessel. They emerged as a little steam. The staircase brightened, and the waiting hand beyond the furnace closed around the gate's latch.

The potter placed the crown inside the furnace. The creature opened its mouth, and blue light filled the workshop without casting a single shadow.

## What the Fire Returned

When the light faded, the crown was gone. On the furnace floor lay a small cup, plain white and warm enough to hold comfortably. Its bottom showed a house with smoke rising from its chimney.

The visitor drank from it. The water tasted of nothing she had lost. Outside, the first ordinary snow of the year began falling over the hills.

She left with the cup inside her closed hand, though the potter had measured it wider than her palm. He watched until the road curved out of sight. Her footprints continued toward the hills, and none turned back.

The last shelf is empty now. Blue dust still gathers on its wood.

Sometimes, before lighting the furnace, the potter hears a crown being set gently upon a table in a distant house. He waits until the sound has ended, then begins his day's work.
